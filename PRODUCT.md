# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Two primary users at a retail store:

- **Cashiers** — scan lottery book barcodes, activate books at their station, run shift-close audits. Using the app quickly, often on a tablet or phone at the counter. They need speed, clarity, and immediate feedback (sound, color, confirmation).
- **Managers / owners** — review stock levels, check catalog, track revenue and audit history, manage shift records. Using the app for bookkeeping and oversight. They need structured data, clear summaries, and enough depth to catch discrepancies.

Both roles share one app and one URL. The manager surface is the same interface — the data just runs deeper.

## Product Purpose

A lottery scratch ticket inventory management system for a retail store. Tracks every physical book of tickets from arrival to retirement: receiving books, activating them at stations, recording position through shift audits, and surfacing data for business bookkeeping.

Success means: the manager knows exactly what stock is where, every shift's ticket count is reconciled, and no book falls through the cracks between received, active, and sold out.

## Brand Personality

Warm, dependable, fast.

Not corporate, not playful. The tone of a well-run small business — friendly enough for a new cashier on their first day, efficient enough for a manager who's done this 500 times. Clean without being cold.

## Anti-references

- Casino or gambling aesthetics: no neon, no jackpot-style color flashes, no slot-machine UI patterns — even though the subject is lottery.
- Generic SaaS dashboard templates: no hero metric cards with gradient accents, no "3 big numbers in a row" stat blocks, no visual filler.
- Government / DMV-style interfaces: no grey forms, no bureaucratic emptiness, no interfaces that feel like compliance obligations.

## Design Principles

1. **Speed at the counter** — audit flows and scan interactions must be zero-friction. Every tap or scan should resolve in under one second of UI. The cashier shouldn't think about the interface.
2. **Role-aware depth** — the same screen can surface a quick action for a cashier and a full data view for a manager. Don't dumb down; layer instead.
3. **Inventory as truth** — what the screen shows must match physical reality. Visual clarity and data integrity matter more than decoration. Ambiguity in a count costs money.
4. **Touch-native first** — built for tablets and phones at the counter, not a desktop admin panel. Targets large, layout vertical, interaction forgiving.
5. **Warm without distraction** — color, motion, and personality serve feedback and hierarchy. They do not draw attention to themselves.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Touch-first: designed for tablet and mobile use at a retail counter
- Large touch targets throughout, especially for scan/audit flows
- High contrast text for counter and floor use (varying ambient light conditions)
- WCAG AA minimum
